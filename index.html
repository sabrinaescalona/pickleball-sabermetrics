Rally Wins Above Replacement Equivalent (rWAR-eq)

An honest, unadjusted prototype metric. Enter rally outcomes or import a rally-level CSV. This is NOT validated individual WAR and is NOT derived automatically from video.

CSV columns: player,partner,opponent,won,match_id. One row per player per rally. won accepts true/false or 1/0. Never interpret this as a causal attribution.

Adjusted Plus-Minus (APM) — experimental

Opponent- and partner-adjusted rally impact using ridge-regularized logistic regression. Includes a separate partnership synergy term and serving-team adjustment. Requires one verified row per rally with all four players. These are not validated WAR estimates.

Required columns: match_id,rally_id,player,partner,opponent,opponent2,won,serve_team. Team A = player + partner; Team B = opponent + opponent2. won is Team A's result (1/0). serve_team is A or B. Minimum 20 rallies and at least 5 distinct players with partner rotation.

Pickleball Wins Above Replacement (PWAR) — experimental

Default: skill-adjusted PWAR. Toggle universal to compare across skill levels. Requires the APM rally records above and ratings for every player. Only rally scoring is supported.

Partnership PWAR — experimental

Uses the APM rally records and ratings from the PWAR section above. Skill-adjusted is the default; universal is available as a toggle. Pair WAR compares two actual players against two replacement players, rather than adding individual PWAR values. Chemistry isolates the modeled partnership interaction.

Next metrics to build

Rally expectancy: a calibrated model of rally-win probability from score, court positions and shot context.

Shot EPA: probability after a shot minus before, using a trained model rather than manual guesses.

Adjusted plus-minus: experimental model available above. Match-context and court-side adjustments are future improvements.

True PWAR: requires a defensible replacement baseline, player-adjusted impact estimates, and conversion to wins under a defined scoring format.

Why opponent adjustment matters

Winning 65% of rallies against beginners isn't necessarily better than winning 52% against strong opponents. A production version will model opponent strength, score state, court side, and partnership effects with uncertainty intervals.
